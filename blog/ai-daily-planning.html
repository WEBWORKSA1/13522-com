Using AI to plan your day without losing control

By the 13522 editorial team · 7-minute read

AI assistants are very good at the tedious parts of planning: turning a messy brain dump into a tidy list, estimating effort, breaking projects into steps and drafting the small stuff. They're much worse at the part that matters most — deciding what is genuinely important to you today. The best workflow uses AI for the first job and keeps you firmly in charge of the second.

Step 1: Dump, don't curate

Start by writing everything on your mind, in any order, without filtering. Include deadlines and rough times if you know them. This takes two or three minutes and empties your working memory.

Step 2: Ask AI to size, not to choose

Paste the list into your assistant with a prompt like:

"Here is my task list. For each item, estimate realistic effort in minutes and label it Big (90+ min), Medium (30–90 min) or Small (under 15 min). Flag anything that looks like a project rather than a task and suggest the next concrete step. Don't prioritise yet."

Notice the last sentence. You're asking for structure, not judgement. The assistant doesn't know that the client call is the thing your quarter depends on, or that the "small" email is actually a delicate conversation.

Step 3: You choose the Big task

Look at the sized list and answer one question yourself: if I only finish one thing today, which one matters most? That's your Big task. Then pick three medium and five small tasks. If you want a sounding board, ask the AI to argue for an alternative — but make the call yourself.

The 13522 planner's Smart Sort does a simple version of step 2 locally in your browser, with no data uploaded. Use it when the list is short and you don't need a conversation.

Step 4: Let AI shrink the small tasks

Small tasks are where AI saves the most time. Drafting a polite decline, summarising a long thread, turning meeting notes into action items or writing a first-pass reply can turn a 15-minute task into a 3-minute review. Batch all five small tasks into one block and use your assistant as a co-pilot.

Step 5: Use AI to unblock the Big task, not to do it

For the Big task, ask for a plan rather than a finished product: "Break 'write the pricing page' into steps I can do in a 90-minute sprint." You'll get a checklist that removes the start-up friction. The thinking — and the quality — stays yours.

AI schedulers: when they help

AI calendar tools that automatically place tasks around meetings can be genuinely useful for people with fragmented days. They work best when you've already decided what goes in the Big slot. Let the tool find the best time; don't let it decide the priority. See our AI tools directory for options.

Privacy and good habits

  • Don't paste confidential client data, passwords or personal health information into general-purpose assistants unless your organisation has approved the tool for that use.
  • Verify any facts, figures or deadlines the AI adds — assistants can be confidently wrong.
  • Keep your evening review human. Reflecting in your own words is where the learning happens.

Used this way, AI turns ten minutes of planning into three, and makes the five small tasks almost disappear — while the decision about what makes today count stays exactly where it belongs: with you.
