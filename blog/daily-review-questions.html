The 5-minute evening review: 7 questions that improve tomorrow

By the 13522 editorial team · 5-minute read

The evening review is the most skipped and most valuable part of any planning system. Five minutes at the end of the day closes open loops, turns today's experience into tomorrow's better plan, and gives you permission to stop thinking about work.

Why it matters

Unfinished tasks tend to stay active in your mind until you make a plan for them. A short review that decides what happens next to each open item lets your brain let go. It also means tomorrow morning starts with action rather than decisions.

The seven questions

  1. Did I finish my Big task? If not, what got in the way?
  2. What carries over? For each unfinished item: carry, delegate or drop.
  3. Has anything carried over three times? If so, rewrite it, split it or remove it.
  4. What took longer than expected? Adjust your estimates for similar tasks.
  5. What gave me energy today, and what drained it?
  6. What is tomorrow's Big task? Write it as an outcome.
  7. Is there anything I need to tell someone before tomorrow?

You won't need all seven every day. Questions 1, 2 and 6 are the core; the others are for when something felt off.

The morning partner

The evening review pairs with a three-minute morning check: look at your calendar, confirm the nine tasks still make sense, and write one sentence — "Today is a win if…". Then start sprint one.

Weekly version

On Fridays, add five minutes: look back across the week's scores, spot patterns in what kept slipping, and choose next week's Big tasks. Over a month, these short reviews compound into a much clearer picture of how you work best.
