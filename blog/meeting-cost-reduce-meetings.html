What meetings really cost — and 6 ways to cut them

By the 13522 editorial team · 6-minute read

Meetings are one of the largest costs in most organisations that never appear on a budget line. Six people at a blended cost of $55 an hour in a one-hour weekly meeting cost $330 every week — over $15,000 a year — before counting the time it takes everyone to refocus afterwards.

Calculate it

The formula is simple: attendees × hourly cost × hours × occurrences. Try our meeting cost calculator with your own numbers; most people are surprised by the annual total for their recurring meetings.

The hidden cost: fragmentation

A meeting at 10:30 doesn't just cost an hour. It turns a three-hour morning into two short fragments, neither long enough for deep work. That's why the 13522 method treats a one-hour meeting as a medium task and encourages teams to cluster meetings away from shared focus windows.

Six ways to cut meeting load

  1. Default to 25 or 50 minutes. Parkinson's law applies to meetings too.
  2. No agenda, no meeting. If the organiser can't write three bullet points, it isn't ready.
  3. Replace status meetings with async updates. A daily written "Big task + blocker" post replaces most of them.
  4. Invite fewer people. Share notes with those who only need to know the outcome.
  5. Audit recurring meetings quarterly. Cancel them all for a week and only restore the ones people miss.
  6. Protect focus windows. Agree team-wide meeting-free blocks and respect them, especially if you're the manager.

Make the meetings you keep count

End every meeting by confirming who does what by when. Those actions become small or medium tasks in the right people's next 1-3-5 lists — rather than vague intentions that resurface at the next meeting.
